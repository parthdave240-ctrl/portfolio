Making TigerSight the intelligence layer above ERP and TMS

Led positioning, messaging and content for tigersight.ai, and guided the designers through the build, from site architecture to the copy on every section.

“TigerSight, Freight Tiger’s newest and most strategic product, had no clear home in the existing site, and needed to compete for attention against project44 and India’s established visibility players while explaining a genuinely complex, 20-plus-agent product to very different buyers.”

The positioning

The category claim the whole site ladders up to

Logistics’s next chapter

Not another visibility tool. TigerSight is positioned by where it sits: the third layer of the stack, above the systems a buyer already runs.

TigerSightIntelligence layer

Reads live freight data, runs the routine work, and surfaces only what genuinely needs a person.

Freight Tiger TMSExecution layer

Runs the freight and produces the live data the intelligence layer reads.

ERPSystem of record

Orders, inventory and financials, left exactly where they are.

freighttiger.comOperational credibility: TMS, Marketplace, company story.
tigersight.aiThe vision: a separate domain, so the AI story can be more ambitious than a TMS site allows.

The website, built as one argument

tigersight.ai is a single long page with one conversion goal. Every section answers the next question a buyer would ask, in order, and every section ends at the same place: Book a demo.

  1. HeroNames the category in four words, “Logistics’s next chapter”, and says who it’s for: shippers, buyers and procurement teams.
  2. Trust stripRecognisable logos before any product claim, so the ambition reads as credible.
  3. Agent libraryMakes “AI” concrete: 20+ agents, four shown working, the full library offered on request.
  4. Platform modulesEight jobs from Planning to Backhaul Matching, each tagged by the buyer it serves.
  5. OutcomesOperational numbers, not model names: empty miles, follow-up calls, team productivity.
  6. IndustriesFMCG, steel and infrastructure, automotive and cement, so each buyer finds their own network.
  7. TechnologyThe three-layer stack, answering “where does this fit with what we already run?”
  8. Team & securityExperience, live training data from Freight Tiger’s network, and data that stays under the customer’s control.
  9. Book a demoOne primary CTA, repeated at every section break, with business enquiries as the secondary path.

Section order of the live homepage at tigersight.ai.

The messaging system

A complex product, explained with a few repeatable patterns, so every section of the site sounds like the same company.

Category line
Logistics’s next chapter.

Short enough to own, big enough to separate TigerSight from tracking and visibility tools.

Stack line
The intelligence layer that runs as the third layer of your stack.

Answers the integration question before a buyer asks it: nothing gets ripped out.

Agent pattern
One plain line on what it does, then a working example.

Sentry ranks exceptions by impact. Voice agents call idle drivers and update the ETA. No jargon, no model names.

Module pattern
Step in only when an exception genuinely needs a person.

Every module headline states the outcome for the operator, then tags who it serves.

Ground TeamCXO
Proof pattern
Numbers a buyer can check in their own operation.

Empty miles from 20% to 8%, 80% fewer manual follow-up calls, 3x team productivity.

How it came together

01

Gave the AI product its own domain and a bolder claim

Launched TigerSight on tigersight.ai, positioned as ‘logistics’s next chapter’, so the AI story could be more ambitious than a TMS site allows while staying clearly part of Freight Tiger. The parent site’s “AI” nav item feeds it directly.

02

Explained it as a stack position, not a feature list

Positioned TigerSight as the third layer above the ERP and the TMS, reading live freight data, automating routine work and surfacing only what genuinely needs a person, so buyers understood where it fits before what it does.

03

Planned the site as one argument with one CTA

Set the section order so each block answers the buyer’s next question, from category claim to trust, agents, modules, outcomes, industries, stack and security, and briefed the designers to end every section at Book a demo.

04

Made the agents concrete, not abstract

Built the agent library around specific examples: Sentry ranks and auto-resolves exceptions, Long Stoppage Voice Agents call an idle driver and update the ETA, Vehicle Procurement Voice Agents call every transporter on a lane at once.

05

Wrote eight modules for two buyers at once

Planning through Backhaul Matching, each module pairs today’s pain (spreadsheets, detention found weeks later on an invoice, attention going to whoever calls loudest) with what TigerSight does instead, tagged Ground Team or CXO so each buyer sees their own stake.

06

Carried the same story into the enterprise pitch

Reframed the agent library as one intelligence layer an enterprise can audit: guardrails, a freight-grade data spine, a model-agnostic AI platform and decisions that act, led by measured outcomes instead of model names.
