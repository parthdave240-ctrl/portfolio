D2CLIFECYCLE / EMAIL / WHATSAPP

Rebuilding the Rotimatic customer lifecycle across email, WhatsApp and delivery updates

“A $1,400-plus machine with an 8 to 10 week delivery wait, and a program that was one cart email and a delivery notice sent after pickup. Customers waited in silence, and about 20 to 22% of orders were cancelled.”

The 60-second version

Problem
One abandoned-cart email, carts going straight to sales calls, and a delivery email only after pickup. Customers waited 8 to 10 weeks in silence and about 20 to 22% of orders were cancelled, largely because the delivery date wasn't clear.
What I built
Cart nurture by urgency window, a ten-stage "Where is my Rotimatic?" tracker in weekly emails, excitement emails during the wait, delivery updates, onboarding, referral, warranty recovery and accessory upsell.
Channels
Email, WhatsApp (cart campaigns), transactional email (delivery updates), and inside sales and chat, working as one system rather than four separate tools.
Proof
Warranty attach rate up from 43 to 44% to 52 to 53%. Email and referral drove about 24% of revenue from about 4% of sessions. The calling and chat layer sold 779 machines in the months tracked.

Where it started

The email program was one abandoned-cart email plus a few transactional delivery updates. Everything between "add to cart" and "parcel picked up" was silent, and so was everything after the box arrived.

Gap 1

Carts weren't nurtured

After the single cart email, abandoners went directly to inside sales. The calling team's relationship-first approach worked, but the call was doing work a nurture sequence should have done first.

Gap 2

The wait was invisible

Delivery took 8 to 10 weeks and the delivery email went out only when the parcel was picked up. Roughly 20 to 22% of orders were cancelled, largely from not knowing the delivery date.

Gap 3

No post-purchase journey

Nothing taught a new owner to unbox, make a first roti or care for the machine, and nothing asked for feedback, reviews or referrals.

Gap 4

No view of why people left

There was no structured way to learn why a visitor who added to cart did not buy.

Channel roles

Each channel had a distinct job. The point was not more channels, it was the right channel at the right moment.

Email

The relationship channel

Where the story, proof and education live: cart nurture, the wait series, onboarding, advocacy and warranty. Email converted at 1.71%, well above paid social.

WhatsApp

The fast nudge

Abandoned-cart campaigns ran on WhatsApp alongside email, triggered automatically the moment a cart was abandoned, for the shopper who sees a message sooner than they open an email.

Transactional email

The delivery promise

Delivery updates: out for delivery and the updates after. This is the channel people rely on once they have paid, so it carries the delivery clarity.

Inside sales and chat

The human layer

Calls and messages to high-ticket abandoners within 2 hours, a "request a callback" option, and live chat for real-time questions. The brief was relationship over sales: build trust and remove friction around financing, delivery and warranty.

Systems and orchestration

How data moved between tools, and how the channels were kept from stepping on each other.

Orchestration principles

  • One job per send. Each message does one thing: remind, reassure, educate, ask or offer. No send repeats the last one.
  • Time decides the message. A cart from this week and a cart from last quarter get different emails, not the same one later.
  • Speed for high intent. Email and WhatsApp trigger automatically at abandonment, and high-ticket carts get a human call within 2 hours, while hesitation is still real.
  • Triage before the human. An AI-assisted layer routes sales queries to callers and support issues to chat, so each team works its own queue.
  • Relationship over discount. Callers were briefed to build trust and answer real questions on financing, delivery and warranty, rather than run a discount-led close.
  • Status from the source. The tracker reflects the production system rather than a manual update, so what customers see matches what is happening.
  • Lead capture feeds the same flows. Visitors who weren't ready to buy could trade contact details for a recorded demo and a $50 code, and then entered the Klaviyo nurture rather than bouncing.

Testing and learnings

Frame the decision, not the discount

"Own Rotimatic for $1,599 today only" beat "Save $200" on click-through and conversion, and the same reframing informed email and call scripts. The founder-launch email, a plain personal note, reached a 12.8% click rate, roughly 20 times the campaign median.

Units beat clocks

A countdown worked for one-off events but lost pull on recurring promotions. "Only 51 units left at this price" outperformed it on festive sales.

Fix the decision, not the price

Extended warranty attach was flat at 43 to 44%. An audit found the warranty choice itself was badly designed. After the checkout redesign and a three-email recovery flow, attach reached 52 to 53%.

Silence was the cancellation driver

The 20 to 22% cancellation problem was about clarity on the delivery date, not price or acquisition, so the fix was communication rather than another discount.

Sales campaigns (kept separate)

Most email revenue came from sales email: abandoned-cart offers and festive campaigns such as Black Friday, Christmas and New Year. They are reported separately so lifecycle work isn't credited with promotional revenue.

What this shows

Diagnose the real leak

Cancellations came from silence, not price, so the fix was communication.

Connect systems

Customer-facing status tied to the production and order system.

Segment by behaviour and time

Different messages for different urgency windows.

Use the right channel

Email for story, WhatsApp for speed, transactional for the delivery promise, people for high intent.

Retention feeds growth

Onboarding leads to reviews and referral, the channels that already converted best.

Keep sales separate

Lifecycle and promotion measured as different jobs.
