01 / What I do

What I do as GTM operator.

A lean operational setup in four stages. You can also start with a single channel or a specific problem.

Stage 1 of 4Foundation

Stages

Stage 1: Foundation

Know who you sell to, why you win and what to say, before you spend on anything else.

Step 1

Audit and plan

A clear baseline, what to fix first, and goals for 3, 6 and 12 months.

Step 2

Ideal customer

Your best buyers, what triggers a purchase, and who to say no to.

Step 3

Positioning

Pick your anchor, weigh options ranked by risk, and lock the exact words for what you are and who it is for.

Step 4

Messaging

The words that explain your value and why you win, the same everywhere buyers look.

You leave with: ICP, positioning and messaging your team agrees on.

Stage 2: Build

Make your assets and website match, and set your channel strategy.

Step 1

Marketing and sales assets

The website, product pages, decks and creatives buyers see, rebuilt to match your positioning.

Step 2

Website fixes and landing pages

Fix what costs you conversions on your site, and build landing pages that match your positioning.

Step 3

Growth engine

Pipeline and channel strategy: the channels you use, how each performs (paid, email, SEO and more), and where to focus.

You leave with: Assets and a website that match, and a clear channel strategy.

Stage 3: Growth execution

Put the growth system to work: your channels, AI workflows and funnel.

Step 1

Channel execution

Run the channels and campaigns from your growth plan, week to week.

Step 2

AI readiness and workflows

Check how ready your team is for AI, then build workflows that take repeat work off your plate.

Step 3

Funnel and conversion setup

Fix and connect the path from first visit to customer: lead capture, forms, add to cart and checkout.

You leave with: A running growth system, with AI workflows and a connected funnel.

Stage 4: Measure and hand over

Track what is working, then hand everything over so your team can run it.

Step 1

Reporting and planning

Dashboards that show what is working and what to do next.

Step 2

Handover

Everything documented and handed to your team, with playbooks they can run.

You leave with: Dashboards that guide decisions, and a clean handover to your team.

Operator

What you get as Operator.

A 6-month minimum, priced to the scope of execution. All 12 steps of your GTM, in order, each built on the last.

Deliverables, in four parts

Part 1: Foundation Steps 1 to 4

  • Audit and plan: a GTM assessment, what to fix first, goals for 3, 6 and 12 months, and a roadmap
  • ICP: your ideal company and personas, intent signals and anti-ICP
  • Positioning: your primary anchor, 4 to 6 options ranked by risk, and the exact words for what you are, who it is for and the value you deliver
  • Messaging: the master messaging framework, consistent across your homepage, ads and sales or product pages

Part 2: Build Steps 5 to 7

  • Marketing and sales assets: website, product pages, decks, creatives and case studies rebuilt to match your ICP, positioning and messaging
  • Website fixes and landing pages: fixes for what costs you conversions, and landing pages that match your positioning
  • Growth engine: pipeline and channel strategy: the channels you use, how each one performs (paid, email, SEO and more), where to focus and how they fill your pipeline

Part 3: Growth execution Steps 8 to 10

  • Channel execution: running the channels and campaigns from your growth plan, week to week
  • AI readiness and workflows: a check on how ready your team and tools are for AI, then workflows that take repeat work off your plate
  • Funnel and conversion setup: lead capture, forms, add to cart and checkout connected, so every visitor and lead is captured and followed up

Part 4: Measure and hand over Steps 11 and 12

  • Reporting and planning: dashboards, weekly and monthly review, and plan adjustments
  • Handover: everything documented and handed to your team, with playbooks they can run
Every monthWeekly and monthly reporting against the goals set in the audit, and a monthly review that adjusts the plan.
